Step 1 of 5

What are you building?

Your archetype determines your capex structure, your route to scale, and which financing instruments exist for your category. Two companies in the same climate sector can have completely different capital stack options if their business models differ. Identify yours before anything else.

Why it matters — examples
A Cactos sells battery units — scales by replication. Each unit finances separately. NOAK does not need a new FOAK capital raise.
B Solar Foods Factory 01 — one facility, one output. The whole company depends on one project financing round closing.
C Vianode sells anode material to many battery makers — manufacturing logic, competitive market pricing, multi-buyer offtake.
D Stegra — structurally indivisible. Requires a pre-FID coalition of equity partners. Cannot be financed by any single actor alone.

Step 2 of 5

What is your inherent risk profile?

Five dimensions determine which capital providers can structurally engage. These are fixed by your technology and market context — you cannot argue your way past them. You can only resolve them through derisking actions. Rate each dimension honestly based on your situation today.

What each dimension means
T Technology novelty — how novel is the process? VH = new science never operated at commercial scale.
P Price vs alternatives — can output compete on cost without a green premium or subsidy today?
F Feedstock — is input supply contracted, at stable price, at commercial scale?
M Market demand — does a contractable buyer pool exist at the required volume and price?
I Infrastructure — does the project depend on grid, pipeline, or regulatory framework that does not yet exist?
Rate each dimension — click to select
⚠ Pre-FOAK gate violation — do not proceed to capital raising.
M=VH and I=VH simultaneously means the market structure must change before any capital stack can be assembled. No instrument exists that can bridge both gaps. Focus on ecosystem orchestration, regulatory advocacy, and demand creation first. Climeworks Mammoth is the canonical case.
Step 3 of 5

What must you resolve, and in what order?

For each blocking risk dimension there is a specific derisking action and an evidence threshold that capital providers accept as proof it is resolved. The sequence is not optional — approaching a provider before their blocking condition is resolved does not get you "not yet." It closes the door permanently with that provider.

Sequence logic — Solar Foods Factory 01
1st Demo data resolves T=HIGH. Business Finland grant anchors the stack and signals to the next layer.
2nd IPCEI grant committed — resolves P=HIGH by covering the cost gap to fossil alternatives. Finnish Climate Fund equity follows.
3rd Offtake negotiations advance — resolves M=MED. Enables project financing discussion for Factory 02.

Step 4 of 5

Which capital pools unlock at each derisking gate?

Each resolved risk dimension opens a specific capital pool. That pool's commitment then signals to the next one. The stack assembles in sequence — the cheapest capital enters last. Every month you stay equity-funded is a cost disadvantage on your NOAK. The goal is to reach project finance debt as fast as possible.

Three principles on stack sequencing
lock-in The first committed capital pool sets the terms for everything that follows. Grant-as-anchor (Solar Foods) vs grant-as-enabler (Enifer) produce structurally different stacks.
signal Each commitment is a signal to the next pool. DFI equity crowds in commercial debt. Offtaker equity crowds in project finance. ECA cover crowds in commercial bank participation.
cost VC equity costs 10–40× more than senior project finance debt. Derisking is the mechanism by which you access cheaper capital. It is not a compliance exercise.

Step 5 of 5

What does each provider actually need?

Stated requirements are not revealed requirements. For each provider type there is a specific evidence package, a deal-killer list, and a signal that their commitment sends to the next provider. Knowing the gap between stated and revealed is what changes the outcome of a capital raise.

Stated vs revealed — two examples from deal evidence
EIB Says: bankable offtake required. Reveals: advanced HoT with credible industrial buyer is sufficient at venture debt stage. Full take-or-pay not required.
ECA Says: proven technology. Reveals: independent technical assessor report confirming binary risk off the table is the actual gate — not operating hours or TRL number.
Provider profiles — stated vs revealed requirements
Catalytic grant programs (BEC Catalyst · Innovation Fund · national) First-loss grant
Stated: emission impact, technology readiness, project viability.
Revealed: project readiness checklist is the real gate — site, EPC relationship, execution credibility, and at least indicative commercial traction. Technology proof alone is not sufficient.
Deal-killers: no site identified, founding team with no industrial project experience, no co-investor signal, permitting not started.
What their commitment signals: technology is credible — allows DFI and EIB to engage without repeating the tech assessment.

National DFI equity (Tesi · Norfund · CDP · KfW · BBB) Development finance equity
Stated: additionality, climate impact, viable business model.
Revealed: contracted demand is the deciding variable — not technology novelty. Requires board seat at A and C archetypes. Return target typically 8–12% IRR with longer horizon than VC. Most predictive survival instrument for Manufacturing Factory (C).

ECA-backed senior debt (Finnvera · EKF · Euler Hermes · UKEF · Atradius) Senior guaranteed debt
Stated: proven technology, bankable project structure, experienced EPC contractor, adequate equity cushion.
Revealed: independent technical assessor confirming binary risk off the table is the actual technology gate — not operating hours. Most cost-effective bankability lever in Northern Europe. Converts project-level credit risk to near-sovereign credit quality.
Requires: FEED complete, T resolved via ITA, EPC model selected and contractor engaged, offtake at advanced HoT, site and all permits confirmed, equity cushion ≥20–30% of total project cost.
Deal-killers: binary technology risk, no offtake of any form, no site or permit certainty, equity cushion below threshold.
What their commitment signals: commercial banks will participate alongside or behind ECA cover — the single most powerful crowd-in mechanism for senior debt.
